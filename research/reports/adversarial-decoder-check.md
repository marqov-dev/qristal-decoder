## Headline: REPRODUCED-WITH-CAVEATS

- **Correctness reproduced.** My independent implementation passes every gate (below), and is node-for-node identical to the certified-exact-decoder report's `decode_exact_backward` on 20 identical instances across 7 families (same expansions, same generations, same mode and p*). Admissibility and exactness of the returned argmax (given termination) are proven, not empirical.
- **The T²W² law reproduced on peaky tables** (conf ≥ 0.7–0.8): gen/(0.1·T²·W²) = 0.8–1.0 at T=64/128/200 (W=5) and at W=32 (T=16/32/64); final search 1.0–1.25·D in my seeds. Their own JSON gives 0.074–0.142 on the 11 hard cells; their 70 real W=32 posteriors give median 0.094, max 0.51 (1 outlier). That part of the claim stands.
- **"Independent of p*" does NOT hold for the decoder — it is a property of the peaky family.** Worst case found: **exactly-uniform ("flat") rows — final-search expansions = (W−2)^{T/2} exactly** (×3 per 2 frames at W=5: 53, 161, 485, 1457, 4373, 13121, 39365, 118097 for T=8..22; ×2 at W=4; ×6 at W=8), total generations ≈ 3.3·(1/p*)^0.82, i.e. **the same exponential class as Graves' c/p*** (Graves is 3–4× more nodes on the same instances). Identical counts under exact `Fraction` arithmetic and under ±10⁻³ multiplicative noise (near-ties, no exact ties), so this is structural, not a float/tie artefact. The mechanism is fully explained and verified exactly: expansions = number of proper prefixes of the (W−1)(W−2)^{D−1} tied labellings, every one of which the Σ_t split bounds *strictly* above p*.
- **Between the two extremes the cost interpolates smoothly with confidence**: gen ∝ (1/p*)^α with α = 0.08 (peaky conf 0.8), 0.17 (conf 0.6), 0.31 (conf 0.5), 0.24 (uniform-random rows, W=5), 0.50 (near-tie with 3-frame spread), 0.82 (flat). Equivalently gen ∝ T^β with β = 2.5, 3.2, 4.6, 3.5, 3.7, 7.3. The "final search = D expansions" claim fails off the peaky family: up to 1342·D (degenerate, T=96), 621·D (conf 0.5), 253·D (spread-pair), 123·D (near-tie spread 3).
- Provable statement (see §4): every search (final and each of the T·(W−1) sub-searches) expands at most the number of prefixes of labellings whose probability is within a factor T of that search's mode. The decoder is polynomial exactly when that count is; on flat/near-flat tables it is (W−2)^{Θ(T)} and the decoder is exponential.

## 3. the certified-exact-decoder report code diff and gate audit

- **Behaviour:** `../code/certified-exact-decoder/bounds.py::decode_exact_backward` and `../code/adversarial-decoder-check/t9core.py::decode_exactbwd` are the same algorithm; on 20 identical instances (peaky 0.8/0.5, uniform-random W=5/8, flat, near-tie, spread-pair, degenerate) expansions, generations, mode and p* agree exactly (`diff.log`). The only implementation differences: they run each sub-search over the full-length table (O(T) per node; mine restricts to frames t..T, halving the constant), and they seed Ĥ with the policy DP (never read). Every attack number above therefore applies to their decoder unchanged.
- **Admissibility gate (`gate.py::gate_admissible`)**: `brute_g(table, t, c)` enumerates every path over frames t..T with π_t = c and groups by `collapse`, so the brute H is the max over ALL nonempty suffix labellings of every length, including those beginning with the same symbol as the prefix's last symbol (that case is handled on the prefix side by the `[u_last ≠ d]` factor in `ok`, correctly). The empty suffix is correctly excluded from H (it is the `p(u)` term of the bound). It tests equality, not just ≥, for the exact-backward H (worst |Δ| 5.6e-16). Coverage limit: T ≤ 6, W ≤ 3, peaky conf ≥ 0.8 only — no low-confidence or flat tables, i.e. exactly the regime where the search does the most work; my (b) gate adds conf 0.6 and W=4 (0 mismatches, exact rationals).
- **Argmax gate (`gate_argmax`, `gate_w.py`)**: brute-force float, 400 + 120 instances as claimed. Two gaps: (1) when the returned label equals the brute-force mode, the returned p* is *not* checked (only checked in the tie branch); (2) the benchmark "wrong" column (`bench.py`) compares against a reference that is the policy/exact-backward decoders themselves (`assert abs(r1[0]-r2[0])`), so "no disagreement in any cell" is self-consistency among three decoders sharing `best_first`, `child` and `bound_from_H`, not a brute-force check. Neither gap hides a bug (my gates check p* and pass), but the report's phrasing "gated exact on 520 brute-force instances" should not be read as covering the large cells.
- **Cap semantics**: `bench.py` caps *expansions per search* (3·10⁴), so "exact-backward itself did not cap in any cell" bounds each of the T·(W−1)+1 searches, not the total; fine for their grid, but my per-total caps are what the flat/degenerate families hit.
- **0.1·T²·W² vs their logs**: `bench_hard.json` gives gen/(T²W²) = 0.074–0.142 on the 11 hard cells, `scale2_w5_c08.log` 0.09–0.20 at T=200–500, `bench_random.json` 0.51–0.91 (random rows, growing with 1/p*: their (1/p*)^0.17 fit), and their 70 real W=32 tables (`real_*.jsonl`, all status ok, ≤ 127 s in C) min 0.002 / median 0.094 / max 0.513 with one 58·D final search. The law is supported by their logs **for peaky and real posteriors**; their own random-row numbers already show it is not universal, which the report states in a subordinate clause but not in the headline.
- **Headline wording**: "expands exactly D nodes ... on every peaky cell with T ≥ 64" — 1.00 in their seeds, 1.0–1.25 in mine; harmless. "Total work is polynomial and independent of p*" — true only on the peaky family; false as a property of the algorithm (§2). The real-audio section (f) added after the report's first version is consistent with my findings: real posteriors are peaky (conf_mean 0.82–0.98) and sit inside the benign regime.

## 4. Complexity statement

**Proven.**
1. Decomposition p(u·v) = Σ_t ok_{v₁}(t−1)·g_v(t) (unique start frame of the (|u|+1)-th segment); B(u) = max(p(u), max_d Σ_t ok_d(t−1)·Ĥ_d(t)) is admissible for any Ĥ ≥ H; with Ĥ = H computed exactly backward (each sub-search reads only later, already-exact H), every search is an admissible best-first search, so the returned labelling is the argmax and each H_c(t) is exact *whenever the search terminates* (it always does: finite tree, pruning at B ≤ incumbent). Termination is guaranteed but not bounded.
2. **Upper bound (new, tight in form).** For any node u, B(u) ≤ n_u·max_v p(u·v), where n_u ≤ T is the number of frames with ok_d(t−1)·H_d(t) > 0 (each term is ≤ p(u·v_t) for the argmax suffix v_t at that frame). Every expanded node has B(u) ≥ p* (an ancestor of the mode is always in the heap until the mode is generated, and afterwards the incumbent is p*). Hence **expansions ≤ |{u : max_v p(u·v) ≥ p*/T}| ≤ Σ_{l : p(l) ≥ p*/T} (|l|+1)**, and the same holds for each sub-search with its own mode and horizon. Verified 0/72 violations by brute force. Total cost ≤ (W−1)·Σ_searches (that count)·O(W·T) arithmetic.
3. **Lower bound / counterexample family.** On flat rows expansions ≥ #proper prefixes of tied labellings with B > p* = (W−1)(W−2)^{D−2}·(1+o(1)), D ≈ 0.45·T, i.e. **(W−2)^{Ω(T)}**, verified exactly. Perturbing the rows by ±10⁻³ leaves the counts unchanged, so the family is open, not measure-zero. The decoder's worst case is therefore exponential in T with base growing with W, and ≈ (1/p*)^0.8 — the same class as Graves.

**Empirical (not proven).** p*-independence / the 0.1·T²·W² law hold on the peaky generator at conf ≥ 0.7 and on the 70 real posteriors (their data). The reason, from bound 2: with per-symbol confidence c the ratio between the mode and a one-symbol-different labelling is ≈ (1−c)/((W−1)c) (1/16 at c=0.8, W=5; 1/6 at 0.6; 1/4 at 0.5), so the number of labellings within a factor T of the mode is ≈ Σ_{j ≤ log T / log(1/r)} C(D, j)·(W−1)^j — a polynomial whose degree grows like log T/log(1/r): quasi-polynomial in T at fixed confidence, and unbounded as r → 1 (flat). The measured exponents β = 2.5 → 3.2 → 4.6 → 7.3 for c = 0.8 → 0.6 → 0.5 → flat track that. **No argument bounds the sub-search cost by a polynomial on peaky tables**; the report's "≈ 5–12 expansions per backward search" is an observation on one generator, and the degenerate family (peaky conf 0.8 with 20 % exactly-uniform frames) produces a 1342·D final search and 23260-expansion sub-searches at T=96 from a table that is 80 % peaky.

**What this means for the quantum-costing thread.** The classical exact baseline on real/peaky posteriors is what the certified-exact-decoder report says (≈ 0.1·T²·W² node builds, each O(W·T)), and beam-vs-certified findings in their §(f) are unaffected. But the baseline cannot be quoted as "p*-independent" in general: for any instance family the honest classical count is Σ_searches |{prefixes of labellings within factor T of that search's mode}|, which is polynomial on peaky tables and (W−2)^{Θ(T)} ≈ (1/p*)^0.8 on flat ones. Any advantage argument should be stated against that count, not against c/p* and not against T²W².
